By four in the afternoon the iron frames of the greenhouse were too hot to touch, and wasps drifted lazily among the wild fennel that had pushed through the flagstones.

Elena had found the key hanging from a rusted nail inside the potting shed—not hidden so much as forgotten, like most things in her grandfather's garden. Half the roof panes were still intact, casting long bands of amber light across rows of clay pots stacked upside down in dusty pyramids.

She set her bicycle against the stone wall and sat on the low brick sill with a thermos of cold mint tea. From here she could hear the regional train horn every forty minutes down in the valley, muted by plum trees heavy with fruit nobody would pick this season.

***

In a corner crate she found three glass jars filled with labeled brass screws and a folded map of the district dated 1984. Someone had circled three creeks in faint blue pencil—places where wild trout used to hold under the alder roots during dry summers.

She poured a cup of tea, waited for the dust motes to settle back into the sunbeams, and unfolded the map across her knees. Even faded, the paper smelled faintly of cedar drawers and summer rain.
